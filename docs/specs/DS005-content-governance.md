# DS005 Content Governance And Evidence Boundaries

## Introduction

This specification defines what the LydiaRX site may claim, how it should discuss future-facing initiatives, and how it should handle areas where public disclosures are intentionally incomplete.

## Core Content

The site must describe LydiaRX AG as a venture studio building venture vehicles, platforms, and infrastructure around genomic AI, regulated agentic AI, secure biomedical collaboration, and automated R&D. The wording must remain technically literate, commercially serious, and defensible.

Pages may describe venture directions, solution areas, research programs, platform ambitions, and partner categories when those statements remain framed as capabilities, focus areas, or build directions. Pages must not make unsupported clinical, regulatory, or deployment claims.

The Genomic AI, Omics Firewall, and Pharma R&D Workbenches narratives must remain research-grade and infrastructure-oriented. They may describe governed workflows, analytics, secure access, simulation, or collaboration patterns, but they must not imply clinical validation, regulatory approval, or production-scale deployments that the repository cannot defend.

The Team page must avoid invented personal identities. When LydiaRX has approved public biographies and portraits on its live public site, the repository may reuse those named profiles directly through concise expandable cards; when disclosure is incomplete, it should fall back to role-based blocks and disclosure-safe descriptions instead of inventing people or credentials.

The Contact and Research pages must favor actionable, disclosure-safe language. Contact routes may be grouped by partnership type, but the named public biographies belong on the Team page rather than being duplicated broadly across contact routes. Those profile bodies should remain plain-text summaries with, at most, a short reference line rather than nested sub-sections or long credential inventories. The Research page may act as a hub for linked public articles and position papers, but those articles must stay concise, defensible, grounded in materials the repository can actually support, and end with real clickable bibliography links rather than placeholder references.

Public-facing content structures must stay natural to read. Marketing-style tables that merely restate narrative copy are discouraged because they read like placeholders rather than business content. When structured presentation is helpful, the site should favor cards, concise grouped sections, or short reference lists; only the specification surfaces themselves may rely on table formatting as a default presentation mechanism.

Interactive public popups or modal-style detail panels may expand business-page cards, but their content must remain concise, readable, and disclosure-safe. They are an aid to progressive disclosure, not a place to smuggle in claims that would be too strong for the page itself.

Public business pages must not surface repository bootstrap inventories, specification matrices, coding-style references, or other implementation-oriented housekeeping content. Those details are legitimate on dedicated technical pages but are out of scope for the public LydiaRX narrative.

## Decisions & Questions

### Question #1: When should the Team page use named biographies instead of role-based placeholders?

Response: Named biographies are appropriate only when LydiaRX has already published those identities and descriptions through an approved public source. In the absence of such source material, role-based disclosure preserves professionalism without fabricating people or publishing unsupported biographies.

### Question #2: Why are the product-domain pages written in research-grade language instead of market-ready product claims?

Response: The supplied vision repeatedly emphasizes governed data, scientific defensibility, and venture formation from research assets. Research-grade language keeps the site aligned with that strategy and avoids overclaiming maturity or regulatory status.

## Conclusion

The LydiaRX site must remain ambitious but defensible. Future content additions must respect disclosure boundaries, avoid unsupported claims, and preserve the evidence-oriented tone defined here.
